# Catch-up and moat analysis

Planning judgments are labelled as such. No claim below relies on hidden competitor architecture.

## Direct threats

| Threat | Why it matters | Strategy OS response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AlgoTest | fast Indian idea-to-paper path and options breadth | match workflow clarity; exceed evidence depth |
| Tradetron | established no-code build, backtest, and deploy loop | keep visual entry simple and refuse opaque scores |
| Streak | broker distribution and scanning | remain provider-neutral and research-deep |
| QuantConnect | mature Universe, portfolio, and multi-asset architecture | use as reference; win on visual workflow and India retail usability |
| Composer | very low-friction AI authoring | keep typed deterministic meaning under AI |
| OpenAlgo | broker breadth and interoperability | do not chase logo count; preserve conformance and integration |

## Copyability

### Weeks

Planning judgment:

- dashboard card;
- basic node;
- indicator wrapper;
- simple screener;
- OI chart;
- Fibonacci tool;
- broker logo;
- marketing landing page;
- backtest summary.

These are not durable differentiation.

### Months

Planning judgment:

- polished graph editor behavior;
- one or two broker adapters;
- options Strategy builder;
- basic optimizer;
- paper dashboard;
- static Workflow templates;
- Dynamic Universe over current data;
- chart alerts.

These require sustained product and operational work but remain reproducible by a competent team.

### Years

Planning judgment:

- exact point-in-time Indian market truth;
- content-addressed Strategy, Universe, data, and evidence continuity;
- honest optimizer trial populations;
- research, paper, and live semantic parity;
- incident-hardened broker conformance;
- account capital reservation and reconciliation;
- causal why-trade and why-not-trade history;
- provider and market-rule change knowledge;
- customer trust after real failure recovery;
- versioned Workflow and candidate performance history.

The time comes from accumulated evidence and operating history, not code volume alone.

## Moat layers

### Distribution

Necessary, copyable, and outside architecture alone.

### Point-in-time market truth

Hard because it needs:

- historical instrument identity;
- listing and delisting;
- rule revisions;
- provider correction lineage;
- data rights;
- durable ingestion.

### Reconstructibility

Hard because every result must retain complete causal identity and an independent check.

### Semantic continuity

Hard because one Strategy meaning must survive authoring, research, paper, and execution.

### Execution reliability

Hard because brokers fail in different ways and edge cases accumulate.

### Workflow and evidence history

Hard after the product retains years of exact decisions, rejections, and outcomes.

### Customer trust

Hard because it grows through correct refusals, visible degradation, and honest recovery.

## Strongest position

Strategy OS should become the system that combines:

    point-in-time inputs
    + one typed Strategy meaning
    + bounded research
    + exact evidence
    + portfolio admission
    + safe deployment
    + execution truth
    + review

It should not become:

- a charting clone;
- a broker-terminal clone;
- a specialist options clone;
- a fundamental portal;
- a black-box AI Strategy generator.

## Product choices that protect the moat

1. One IR, validator, resolver, hash, registry, and component model.
2. Universe, Strategy, Workflow, Deployment, and money remain distinct facts.
3. Every dynamic selection is point-in-time and replayable.
4. Every research search records its population.
5. Every deployment binds exact evidence and capability.
6. Every capital decision is deterministic and explainable.
7. Every provider integration adds conformance knowledge.
8. Every incident becomes a permanent regression.
9. Every advanced surface keeps a calm entry point.
10. Private Strategy logic does not become telemetry or training data.

## What can safely continue

- V1 correctness and foundation closure;
- static Strategy research;
- cross-instrument observation and separate execution target;
- provider role separation;
- five-broker conformance work;
- accepted frontend information architecture;
- paper-first user validation.

## What should wait

- Dynamic Universe product;
- general Workflow builder;
- non-OHLCV data;
- semantic chart artifacts;
- broad OI product;
- international markets;
- MT5;
- Bloomberg;
- meta-strategies;
- marketplace;
- shadow racing.

## Distribution implication

Demonstrate questions and evidence rather than feature lists:

- what changed when costs were included;
- why two simultaneous trades received different decisions;
- how a cross-instrument Strategy avoided look-ahead;
- why today's dynamic candidate qualified;
- how a chart thesis expired.

The product should prove its operating model in public without exposing customer Strategy content.
